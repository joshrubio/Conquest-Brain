# 09 — Reflection & Takeaway Rules

> **Status: v0 — working hypothesis.** The reflection is the part that most defines the Dieck Docs feel and the part easiest to get wrong. Study how Dieck builds and exits his closing reflection in `research/dieck-docs/`, then harden this file.

Covers Part 2 (psychological / human reflection, ~20–30% of runtime) and Part 3 (applied takeaway, final 45–90 s) from [02-content-format.md](02-content-format.md).

## A. The reflection (Part 2)

### A1. Purpose
Turn the case from "a thing that happened" into "a thing about how people work". One central human mechanism, not a list.

### A2. Pick ONE mechanism
Name it precisely. Examples of the register: prueba social, sesgo de confirmación, coste hundido, disonancia cognitiva, indefensión aprendida, pensamiento de grupo, identidad y pertenencia, la necesidad de una narrativa coherente, autoridad, esperanza como sesgo.
- If two mechanisms compete, the weaker one is a single supporting sentence, not a second act.

### A3. Anchor to moments already shown
Every claim in the reflection points back to a specific scene from the narrative the audience just watched. **No new facts here** — if you need a new fact, it belongs in Part 1 with a source.

### A4. Interpretive framing is mandatory
"esto sugiere…", "una lectura posible es…", "los estudios sobre X apuntan a…". Never state interpretation as established fact.

### A5. Sourcing inside the reflection
- Naming a bias/dynamic in general terms: no citation needed.
- Citing a specific study, experiment, theorist, or statistic: full source in `source-log.csv` with a `[S..]` tag, same standard as Part 1.
- Popular-psychology claims ("usamos el 10% del cerebro", "la regla de las 10.000 horas") are checked or cut.

### A6. Do not flatter the audience
Avoid "las personas inteligentes también caen" as a cheap reassurance. If the historiography says the actors were rational given their information, say that — it's more interesting and more honest.

### A7. Tone
Quiet, humane, curious. No "y esto nos enseña que…". No contempt for people who fell for it — the point is that the mechanism is in all of us.

### A8. Length & shape
- ~20–30% of runtime.
- Rough shape: transition out of the narrative → name the mechanism → 2–3 anchored observations → widen to the human general → hand off to the takeaway.

## B. The applied takeaway (Part 3)

### B1. One idea
A single, concrete, usable idea. Not three bullet points.

### B2. "Surgical" link — the test
The takeaway must **follow from the case as told**. Test: *state the takeaway to someone who knows the case — do they nod, or do they say "but that's not what happened"?* If the second, the takeaway is wrong. **Change the takeaway, never the facts.**

### B3. Audience connection, not pandering
Speak to the channel's audiences (entrepreneurs, dreamers, workers, people of faith, immigrants, …) through the *idea*, not by name-dropping them. At most one "si estás construyendo algo…"-style line.

### B4. Hard limits
- No pitch for the membership business.
- No link to the business in Phase 1.
- Soft channel CTA only (subscribe / next episode), kept separate from the idea itself.
- ≤ 90 seconds.

### B5. Where it can come from
The takeaway is decided at brief stage as a *candidate*, tested again after the research dossier, and finalized only when the script is written. It is never the reason the case was chosen ([01-editorial-and-sourcing.md](01-editorial-and-sourcing.md) §6).

## C. Failure modes to catch in fact-check

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| New unsourced fact appears in the reflection | Move to Part 1 + source, or cut |
| Two+ mechanisms, none developed | Pick one; demote the rest |
| Interpretation stated as fact | Add "una lectura posible…" |
| Takeaway needs the facts bent | Rewrite the takeaway |
| Takeaway = disguised business pitch | Cut |
| Moralizing closer ("y esto nos enseña…") | Rewrite quiet |
| Specific study cited with no `[S..]` | Source it or remove the specificity |
