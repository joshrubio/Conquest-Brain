# 01 — Editorial Standard & Sourcing

Treat every episode as publishable journalism, not "content". A viewer should be able to check any claim and find it holds.

## 1. The uncited-claim rule

Every factual statement in a script — dates, numbers, quotes, causal claims, "he said", "the company did" — must trace to a source recorded in `source-log.csv` for that episode. If it cannot be sourced, it is cut or explicitly framed as unknown/disputed on screen ("no hay registro de…", "las fuentes discrepan…").

## 2. Source tiers

| Tier | Examples | Use |
|------|----------|-----|
| **A — Primary** | Court records, official filings, archives, government data, original letters/diaries, patents, corporate reports, verified recordings, first-hand testimony on the record. | Preferred. Any strong claim needs at least one Tier A or B. |
| **B — Quality secondary** | Peer-reviewed work, reputable book by a subject-matter historian/journalist with citations, established newspaper of record reporting with bylines. | Acceptable as main support. |
| **C — Weak secondary** | General-interest articles without sourcing, encyclopedic summaries, documentaries. | Lead generation only. Use to find Tier A/B; never the sole support for a claim. |
| **D — Unusable** | Anonymous blogs, social posts, AI output, forums, content farms, unverifiable "reenactment" docs. | Never cited. |

**Minimum bar per episode:** every load-bearing claim has ≥1 Tier A/B source. Contested points need ≥2 independent sources or are presented as contested.

## 3. Public-record eligibility

Before research starts, the subject must clear [ideas/idea-rubric.md](../ideas/idea-rubric.md):
- Is it a public figure, a historical case, or a company/practice?
- Is there a public documentary record (not just chatter)?
- Are the sources in the public domain / lawfully accessible?

If any answer is no → not eligible. No exceptions for "great story".

## 4. Quotes

- Direct quotes reproduced verbatim from the source, with attribution and date, logged with the exact source location.
- Translations into Spanish: mark as translated ("traducción propia" / cite published translation). Keep the original phrasing available in the dossier.
- No paraphrase presented as a quotation. No "composite" quotes.

## 5. Reflection & interpretation

The psychological/human reflection is **interpretation, and must sound like it** — "esto sugiere", "una lectura posible", "los estudios sobre X apuntan a". Psychological concepts invoked (e.g. sunk cost, learned helplessness, in-group bias) are named accurately and, where a specific study or theorist is cited, sourced like any other claim.

## 6. The applied takeaway

Linked at the very end, surgically, one clear idea. It must **follow from the case**, not be pasted on. If the takeaway needs the facts bent to work, the takeaway is wrong — change it, not the facts.

## 7. Corrections

Post-publication errors: pinned comment + description note + on-screen card if material. Log in the episode's `10-retro.md`. Never silently re-upload without noting the change.

## 8. Fact-check gate

No script proceeds to record until `04-fact-check.md` is complete and signed by someone who did **not** write the script (Usuario 002 writes → Usuario 001 checks, or vice versa). See [templates/fact-check-sheet.md](../templates/fact-check-sheet.md).

## 9. AI use

AI may assist with research navigation, structure, transcription, and drafting. AI output is **never a source** and every AI-surfaced fact is re-verified against a real Tier A/B source before it enters the script.
