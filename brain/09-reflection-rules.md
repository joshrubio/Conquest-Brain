# 09 — Reflection & Takeaway Rules

> **Status: v1 — validated against 6 Dieck Docs transcripts (2026-08-27)** (`research/dieck-docs/structure-analysis.md`). Major v0 → v1 change: the reflection is **not always a discrete final block**. The reference set uses three forms — a final reflection+takeaway, a lesson distributed through the last third, and an open question to the audience. All three are legitimate. Also: the close is smaller than v0 assumed (8–20% of runtime, not 20–30%), and Dieck plants the reflection's mechanism via foreshadowing long before the close.

Covers the **close** (section 3 in [02-content-format.md](02-content-format.md)) in all three of its forms.

## 0. Choose the form in the brief

| Form | When it fits | Reference |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| **A — Reflection + applied takeaway** | A single human mechanism runs through the case; there's one usable idea to hand the viewer. | Grinberg, McDonald's |
| **B — Distributed lesson** | A biography where the subject's own words, choices, or fate carry the meaning; a single closing "para ti" would flatten it. | Steve Jobs, Heath Ledger |
| **C — Open question** | A genuinely contested accountability or conspiracy case; the honest ending is to hand the judgment to the viewer after laying out the evidence. | Titanic, Chernobyl |

The form is a Stage-1 decision, revisited after the research dossier. Don't discover it in the edit.

---

## A. Form A — Reflection + applied takeaway

### A1. Purpose
Turn the case from "a thing that happened" into "a thing about how people work". One central human mechanism, not a list.

### A2. Pick ONE mechanism
Name it precisely: prueba social, sesgo de confirmación, coste hundido, disonancia cognitiva, indefensión aprendida, pensamiento de grupo, identidad y pertenencia, la necesidad de una narrativa coherente, autoridad, la esperanza como sesgo. If two compete, the weaker one is a single supporting sentence.

### A3. Plant it early, pay it at the close
Dieck foreshadows the mechanism during the narrative ("recuerda este rasgo, será importante"). Do the same: seed 2–3 moments in Part 2 that the close will name. The close then *collects* what the viewer already saw.

### A4. Anchor to moments already shown
Every claim in the close points back to a specific scene. **No new facts in the close** — a new fact belongs earlier, with a source.

### A5. Interpretive framing is mandatory
"esto sugiere…", "una lectura posible es…", "los estudios sobre X apuntan a…". Never state interpretation as established fact.

### A6. Name an external authority when you invoke a concept
The reference set does this consistently: Michael Sandel + *Lo que el dinero no puede comprar* (McDonald's); Stanislavski + "actuación de método" (Ledger); "resonancia límbica" as a real neuroscience term (Grinberg). Any specific theory / study / thinker / statistic → full source in `03-source-log.csv` with a `[S..]` tag, same standard as the narrative. Naming a bias in general terms needs no citation; popular-psychology claims ("usamos el 10% del cerebro", "la regla de las 10.000 horas") are checked or cut.

### A7. Don't flatter the audience
Skip "las personas inteligentes también caen". If the historiography says the actors were rational given their information, say that — it's more interesting and more honest.

### A8. Tone
Quiet, humane, curious. State the mechanism; let the viewer draw the "should". Dieck sometimes ends on "sería genial que como sociedad…" — we keep ours shorter and more observational (see [08-tone-of-voice.md](08-tone-of-voice.md) §1).

### A9. Length & shape
8–15% of runtime. Shape: transition out of the narrative → name the mechanism → 2–3 anchored observations → widen to the human general → one applied idea.

### A10. The applied takeaway
- **One idea**, concrete and usable. Not three bullets.
- **The "surgical" test:** state the takeaway to someone who knows the case — do they nod, or say "but that's not what happened"? If the second, the takeaway is wrong. **Change the takeaway, never the facts.**
- Speak to the channel's audiences through the *idea*, not by naming them. At most one "si estás construyendo algo…" line.
- ≤ 90 seconds. Kept separate from the CTA.

## B. Form B — Distributed lesson

### B1. How it works
The reflection is broken into 2–3 pieces, each delivered at the biographical beat that earns it, not saved for the end. Steve Jobs: "unir los puntos" after the dropout arc, "amor y pérdida" after the firing, "la muerte" after the diagnosis. Heath Ledger: the method-acting mechanism is planted 4–5 times and pays off in the final act.

### B2. Rules
- Announce the deferral ("de esto te hablo más adelante, cuando el momento sea el indicado") and keep the promise.
- Each piece still obeys A4 (anchored), A5 (interpretive), A6 (sourced concepts).
- The ending is elegiac or summarizing, **not** a "para ti" — the meaning is already in the viewer's hands.
- If the lesson is the subject's own words (a speech, a diary, an interview): quote verbatim, attribute, source, flag any translation.

### B3. Risk
Distributed ≠ absent. If you can't point to the 2–3 places the reflection lives, you have a narrative with no reflection — fix it before fact-check.

## C. Form C — Open question

### C1. When
Only for cases where responsibility or truth is genuinely unresolved (Chernobyl: system vs individuals; Titanic: accident vs plan). Not a way to dodge a conclusion the evidence actually supports.

### C2. Rules
- Lay out **every** serious position fairly, each with its evidence and its weakness ("no hay pruebas concluyentes de…", "esto sí está documentado…").
- Use the optional "las N teorías / los N responsables" module ([02-content-format.md](02-content-format.md) §2).
- State plainly what *is* established before opening the floor.
- End on a real question to the viewer ("¿quién crees que tuvo más responsabilidad?"), then the CTA coda.
- Never imply a verdict through music, editing, or tone that the script won't defend.

### C3. Emotional coda (optional, any form)
The reference set sometimes ends on a human vignette instead of/after the analysis (Titanic: the Strauss love story + the mausoleum inscription). Allowed when it's sourced and true and doesn't distort the case. Keep it short; don't let sentiment overwrite the facts.

## D. Failure modes to catch in fact-check

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| New unsourced fact appears in the close | Move it earlier + source, or cut |
| Form B but the reflection isn't actually anywhere | Identify the 2–3 beats it should live in; write them |
| Form C used to avoid a conclusion the evidence supports | Switch to A/B and state the conclusion |
| Form C but one position is strawmanned | Give every position its strongest case |
| Two+ mechanisms, none developed (form A) | Pick one; demote the rest |
| Interpretation stated as fact | Add "una lectura posible…" |
| Concept invoked with no named authority / `[S..]` | Name and source it, or drop the specificity |
| Takeaway needs the facts bent | Rewrite the takeaway |
| Takeaway = disguised business pitch | Cut |
| Moralizing closer ("y esto nos enseña que…") | Rewrite observational |
| Emotional coda distorts or outweighs the documented case | Trim or cut |
