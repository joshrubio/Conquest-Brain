# 20 — Experimental Clip Protocol

> **Status: canonical, 2026-09-10.** The **Ensayo** track ([ideas/tracks.md](../ideas/tracks.md))
> is live — an Ensayo idea is proposed, scored, approved and produced like any
> other. This doc **extends [brain/12 §1c](12-available-material-protocol.md)** (the
> citation tier for copyrighted film/TV) from "the rare exception" into a full
> track, and carries the rewrite of [charter](00-project-charter.md)
> non-negotiable #1 that makes it possible (`brain/00` #1 is the canonical home;
> this is the detail). Some pipeline tooling is still to build — see §8.

## 0. Why this exists

Farid Dieck's catalogue is two different things:

1. **Documental de caso real** (Grinberg, Titanic, Chernóbil, McDonald's, Jobs,
   Ledger — `research/dieck-docs/structure-analysis.md` T01–T06). Real cases; film
   clips barely appear. **Conquest already does this** — E001 Hokusai uses zero
   clips. No protocol needed.
2. **Contenido construido sobre una obra con copyright** (T07 *Petróleo
   sangriento* / *There Will Be Blood*; his "Relato y Reflexión sobre películas"
   and PeliCast catalogue). The work carries the episode. **This is what the
   Ensayo track is, and it is not a small step** — it needs a format, a
   charter carve-out, a footage doctrine, a discovery workflow, and an honest
   monetisation expectation. That is this doc.

The research finding in one line: what protects this content is **transformation
that is editorial, not technical** — your reading, your narration, your structure.
In practice this plays out as **stills, not moving clips** (§4.1) — every attempt
so far has replaced the moving excerpt with a still in the edit, which also
means Content ID rarely has anything to match. Filters, mirror-flips and crops
are a required *floor* (§4.3) on whatever you do cite, never the argument; alone
they dodge automated *detection* without strengthening the fair-use case and
they degrade the video.

## 1. The three modes

| Mode | The subject is… | Charter fit | Clip role | Typical CPM (`brain/18`) | Footage (§4) |
|------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|--------------------------|--------------|
| **A — obra como sujeto** | the creative work itself, read for what it does / means | needs the full carve-out (§2) | throughout, as the object of criticism | **C (baja)** — pure arts criticism | stills, §4.1/§4.4 |
| **B — obra como cita** | a real public case / person (unchanged) | fine — subject is unchanged | only when the episode is analysing that specific work as part of the story | inherits the host episode | stills, §4.1/§4.4 |
| **C — obra como suceso** | the **documented public event around a work** — its reception, controversy, litigation, ban, box-office phenomenon, industrial or cultural effect | **strongest of the three** — the event has a public record | brief, as commentary on the work's significance | **B, sometimes A** — culture / business / legal angle | stills, §4.1/§4.4 |

**Mode C is the sweet spot** and should be the default reach for Ensayo: the subject
is a real, documented event (a moral panic, a lawsuit, a studio collapse, a
Parental-Advisory sticker, a national ban), it is Tier A/B sourceable from trade
press / court filings / sales data / scholarship, it monetises like a
business-or-institutional case, and the clips are genuine brief commentary. Prefer
C; reach for A only when the reading itself is the reason the episode exists.

## 2. Charter carve-out — creative works as eligible subjects (Ensayo only)

> [brain/00](00-project-charter.md) non-negotiable #1 has been rewritten to name
> this (under change control, commit history of that file), and E1 of
> [`ideas/idea-rubric.md`](../ideas/idea-rubric.md) carries the fourth eligible
> subject type. This is the detail behind that line.

A **released, publicly distributed creative work** — a film, television series,
video game, album, or book — **and the documented public response to it**
(professional criticism, commercial performance, controversy, litigation,
industrial or cultural effect) **are an eligible episode subject** under
non-negotiable #1, provided **all** of:

- **Public record.** The work has a substantial public record — professional
  criticism, trade-press coverage, sales/box-office data, or academic treatment.
  Not a work knowable only to the founders (that would fail non-negotiable #2 and
  E4 anyway).
- **As an object of commentary.** The episode treats the work **as an object of
  criticism, commentary, or cultural history** — it never borrows the work's
  footage to dress an unrelated story. That stays banned ([brain/12 §1c](12-available-material-protocol.md)).
- **Fiction stays fiction.** Fictional characters and events are discussed **as
  authorial and craft choices** ("la película encuadra a Plainview como…", "los
  guionistas le dan…"), never as real psychology or real events. No invented
  inner monologue for a character presented as analysis of a person.
- **Multiple sources.** The script synthesises **several** secondary sources —
  criticism, interviews, making-of documentation, scholarship. Never a
  reconstruction from the work alone ([brain/04 §5](04-legal-and-ethics.md)).

No special sign-off — an Ensayo idea passes Stage 0 like any other (one signer,
[brain/06](06-production-workflow.md)). This carve-out **does not touch** non-negotiables #2 (no one the founders know),
#3 (every load-bearing claim ≥1 Tier A/B source), or #4 (channel independence).
`research/` analysis of the work still may not be a reconstruction from a single
transcript, and living creators covered critically get the [brain/04 §2](04-legal-and-ethics.md)
defamation care (attribute every negative factual claim; opinion framed as
opinion).

**E1 carries:** *"una obra creativa estrenada y su recepción pública documentada
(solo Ensayo, bajo brain/20)."* R3 (cross-check de material) is satisfied for these
ideas by the §4.0 feasibility check, not by the PD archive worksheet.

## 3. Episode anatomy

### Mode C — obra como suceso
**No change to [brain/02](02-content-format.md).** It is a real-event documentary
whose event happens to be the reception of a work. Standard spine (era → build-up
→ the event beat by beat → aftermath → investigation), standard close forms, clips
handled as Mode-B citations (§4). Most Ensayo clip episodes should be this.

### Mode B — obra como cita
**No change to [brain/02](02-content-format.md).** A normal episode about a real
case that cites a work in one or two beats. The existing §1c rules already cover
it; this doc just routes it through the same logging (§4.7).

### Mode A — obra como sujeto (variant of [brain/02](02-content-format.md) §Episode anatomy)

Calibrated to `research/dieck-docs/structure-analysis.md` **T07** (*There Will Be
Blood*) and **T08** (*Cazadores de canales*). The devices are the channel's
standard ones; what changes is the spine and the density of the reading.

| Section | How it differs from the standard (Documental) spine |
|---------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| **Cold open** | **Thesis first, abstract** — state the reading's claim before naming the work ("Timmy juró nunca ser como sus papás… lo difícil es no convertirte en ellos"). Then name the work and the criticism it makes, and **announce the payoff to the viewer** ("si pasaste tu infancia viendo caricaturas, aquí hay respuestas…"). An *in-media-res* open on one charged scene is fine (T08 opens on the dystopian future, then rewinds) — never a plot twist used as bait. **No spoiler warning** — the episode is openly a full reading of the work, ending included; that's the format, not a disclosure. |
| **Bumper** | Unchanged. |
| **Pivote** | The work's **context**: who made it, when it landed, what it was answering — the era and the author, not the plot yet. The first `[EXPLICADOR]` usually sits here (auteur theory, the Hays Code, method acting, the psychological or philosophical frame the reading needs). |
| **Spine (`NARRATIVA`)** | The work's structure — acts / arc / key sequences — is the **scaffold**, narrated in the **present tense**, scene by scene. But the **through-line is the reading, not a plot recap.** Rule of thumb: if you can delete the narrator's interpretation and still follow the story, it's a recap — cut it back. |
| **Reflexión as constant interlude** | After **most** sequences, step back and read it — one or two sentences that (a) name what the scene shows about the mechanism and (b) **bridge fiction → the viewer's own life** ("y esto no solo les pasa a los niños, también a los adultos… las relaciones románticas"). This is the T07/T08 signature and the reason the episode exists. Sits between close-form A and B — see [brain/09](09-reflection-rules.md) §"Ensayo". |
| **First person, mandatory** | "cuando yo era niño…", "a mí me ha pasado…", "creo yo", "dos razones se me ocurren". The reading is *a* reading, offered by a person, not a verdict. |
| **The meta move (optional)** | When it's true and not forced: the viewer is having the same realization *watching the episode* that the character has *in the work* (T08: Timmy learns from television; we learn from watching Timmy). Powerful once; never twice; never manufactured. |
| **Self-catalogue interlink (optional)** | "si viste mi análisis de *X*…" — 1–2 per episode max, **only** to real published Conquest episodes, only where the cross-reference actually adds something. Builds a corpus and retention; overused it's padding. |
| **Making-of as evidence** | Production history — early drafts, director interviews, cut scenes, the writers' room — is *primary-source support for the reading* (T08: "en los primeros bocetos los papás no tenían rostro" → the parents are blurry because that's how Timmy sees them). This is what satisfies §2's "synthesises several sources, not a reconstruction from the work alone." Every such claim is `[S..]`. |
| **One mechanism, tracked** | Planted in the cold open, traced through every act, paid at the close. `[PROMISE]`/`[PAY]` still required — the thematic thread is the promise. |
| **The reading is sourced — and sourcing it is part of writing it** | Writing a Mode-A / suceso reading **is** a research task, done at Stage 4 by whoever drafts the script. For every load-bearing psychological, philosophical, or religious claim in the analysis: **find who has said this well** — a named researcher, a study, a philosopher, a tradition — read enough of the source to quote it accurately, and attach a `[S..]`. Prefer `research/citation-shelf.md` (already verified); when the claim needs someone new, do the lookup, add the row to the shelf, cite it. A reading beat ships either with an authority + `[S..]` or as an explicit general observation ("una lectura posible…") — never as an unsourced assertion in the narrator's voice (the T07 failure). Full rules: [brain/09](09-reflection-rules.md) §"Ensayo" + §A6. |
| **Citations are spread and story-motivated — never a block** | T08's five authorities land *one at a time*, each at the scene that raises its idea (the fantasy/reality one when a child imitates TV; the parasocial one when the boy calls the characters his friends). **Do the same:** at most **one named authority per ~3–4 min** of runtime, each **set up by the plot beat right before it** and handed back to the story right after. A `## NARRATIVA` sub-section that is three researchers back-to-back is a lecture — break each name out to the scene it explains. If two ideas genuinely belong to the same beat, cite one and make the other a general observation. |
| **Close** | Forms A/B, four registers, [brain/09](09-reflection-rules.md). Proportionally **larger** than a Documental close (the reading *is* the point) — but the [brain/08 §1](08-tone-of-voice.md) correction stands, and T07 *and* T08 both fail it: **state the mechanism, do not preach.** "Podemos tomar este cierre como una advertencia… deberíamos…" is the failure mode. Name the tension; stop. |
| **CTA** | Unchanged ([brain/02 §4](02-content-format.md)). `comentar` fits — a reading invites a counter-reading. |

`tools/script_review.py` keys sections off the canonical heading keywords — Mode A
still uses `COLD OPEN` · `BUMPER` · `PIVOTE` · `CONTEXTO` · `NARRATIVA` (the
scaffold) · `CIERRE` · `CTA`.

**Length:** target the same 15–25 min as Documental. The plot scaffold is dense
and fast; the reading is what fills the runtime. If a beat sheet is mostly recap
with a thin reading on top, the episode isn't ready.

## 4. Footage doctrine

### 4.0 Feasibility check (Stage 0 — satisfies rubric R3 for Ensayo)
Three lines in the idea-pool row, no worksheet:
1. **Lawful copy.** Is there a way to obtain the work legally (owned disc, bought
   digital, a subscription that can be screen-captured)? Name it.
2. **Reading sources.** Are there **≥3 Tier A/B** secondary sources for the reading
   — criticism, interviews, making-of, scholarship? (These also satisfy E3.)
3. **Footage plan.** Does the reading plausibly work as cited stills (§4.1/§4.4)?
   A rough "yes" is enough at Stage 0; the shotlist resolves it beat by beat.
Mark `Rights` on the worksheet row as `cita — crítica/comentario (fair use)`.

### 4.1 Stills, not moving clips
A frame still (screenshot) under narration is far lower-risk than a moving clip
with audio — Content ID rarely matches stills, and a still is inherently more
transformative. **In practice, moving excerpts don't survive the final cut** —
every attempt so far has been replaced by a still in the edit. So: **build the
visual track from stills.** The "motion only when the motion is what you're
analysing" exception (a specific cut, a camera move, a performance beat) stays
on the books for the rare case it's genuinely needed, but don't plan around it
and don't build tooling for it (§6) until it actually happens.

### 4.2 Audio is a separate copyright — always replace or duck
Never leave a music cue or a line reading playing clean. Original audio is ducked
hard under narration or replaced entirely. A film's **score** is its own Content
ID surface; a clean 8-second musical moment draws a claim by itself.

### 4.3 Never load-bearing + the minimum treatment
A copyrighted excerpt never carries a beat alone. **Two things always apply:**

1. **Editorial** — narration runs over it, and the beat is about *your point*, not
   the clip. Remove the narration and the beat collapses. This is the
   transformation that matters.
2. **Technical minimums** — every cited excerpt (a still, in practice — §4.1) gets
   **all** of: graded into the house look ([brain/03](03-brand-identity.md)),
   cropped / reframed (not the full original frame), cut into the case-file
   device, and a slow push or reframe move over it (a still is never static —
   [brain/11 §2.4](11-visual-rhythm.md)). A mirror-flip or a punch-in on the
   specific element you're discussing is encouraged where it reads naturally.
   On the rare moving excerpt, add **audio replaced or ducked hard** (§4.2). These
   are a **floor, not the argument** — [§0](#0-why-this-exists) stands: filters
   alone don't make fair use, and a reviewer reads "mirror-flipped, sped up, no
   commentary" as evasion. Do both, or don't use the excerpt.

A raw excerpt playing clean is out — same bar as [brain/12 §1b](12-available-material-protocol.md).

### 4.4 Budgets (soft ceiling — the real limit is 4.3 applied beat by beat)

Stills carry no hard cap by count — each one still transformative (graded,
cropped, under narration, moving per brain/11 §2.4), logged, credited. A
40-minute slideshow of someone else's frames is substitution, not commentary,
regardless of count — if a Mode-A edit is mostly cited stills back to back with
nothing else, that's the signal the reading isn't carrying the episode, not a
budget to raise.

On the rare moving excerpt (§4.1 exception): ≤ ~10 s per excerpt, ≤ ~60 s total
per episode (= the original §1c ceiling) — kept here as a boundary in case the
exception is ever used again, not as active guidance.

### 4.5 PD-first still applies
Films with lapsed or non-renewed copyright (pre-1929 outright; much of pre-1964
US film never renewed — large corpus on archive.org) carry **no claim** and are
used like any archival source. Check status per title first. Trailers are often
released for promotion but are **not** PD — treat as any other studio footage.

### 4.6 Discovery vs. the published still — two different questions

**The source of the bytes changes neither the fair-use analysis nor the Content
ID outcome.** Fair use is about *your* use — transformative commentary, amount,
market effect — not where you got the frame. A screenshot pulled via clip.cafe
and the same frame grabbed from your own Blu-ray get the identical analysis. So
the rule is not "never an online source"; it's:

**Discovery — unrestricted.** To *find* the scene, the timecode, the exact line:
clip.cafe, PlayPhrase, YARN, getyarn, OpenSubtitles, a documentary fragment
someone posted, your own `.srt` — use whatever's fastest. This is research, not
the asset itself.

**The published still — best obtainable quality, and log it.** In order of
preference: (1) a frame grabbed from a source you can lawfully access (owned
disc, bought digital, a paid subscription) — cleanest provenance, best quality;
(2) a still from a clip-service pull — legally no different, use when (1) is
impractical; **paying clip.cafe buys convenience, not a licence** — it grants no
rights, don't treat a subscription as clearance; (3) a frame from a YouTube/
social fragment — last resort, lower quality, can carry a second party's Content
ID claim stacked on the studio's.

Grabbing a single frame is one `ffmpeg -ss <time> -frames:v 1` call — no
dedicated tool needed. `tools/scene_locator.py` (indexing a local `.srt` against
`06-shotlist.md` to find the timecode) stays a nice-to-have, not built until a
real episode actually needs to locate quotes across many works at once — see §6.

`pull_assets.py` is untouched — its `archive` source stays PD-only (Met, Commons);
a cited still is grabbed by hand (`ffmpeg -ss <time> -frames:v 1`) into
`assets/cite/` and logged as `cita` (§4.7), same as any other manually-sourced asset.

### 4.7 Logging
Every excerpt and every still: a `06-shotlist.md` row and a `CREDITS.md` line,
`Rights` = `cita — crítica/comentario (fair use)`, with **title, year,
distributor, and the scene / timecode identified**, plus one line of rationale
(commentary/criticism on this specific work) in `07-assets.md`. Same as [brain/12 §1c](12-available-material-protocol.md).

### 4.8 Absolutes (no mode, no exception)
Never anything that could pass as a fabricated document or record. Never a
photoreal AI stand-in for a real identifiable person ([brain/15](15-ai-illustration-protocol.md)
governs regardless). No song lyrics reproduced on screen or in narration
([brain/04 §4](04-legal-and-ethics.md)).

## 5. Monetisation expectation — a haircut, not zero

Since cited excerpts are stills in practice (§4.1), and Content ID rarely
matches stills, most Ensayo episodes should see little to no claim activity at
all. The haircut case below is for the rare moving-excerpt exception: it would
draw Content ID claims that redirect ad revenue **on the clip-bearing portions**
to the rightsholder — a haircut on the episode's RPM, not a wipe, since the
talking-head, graphics, and narration-over-stills stretches (most of the
runtime) still earn normally.

Three things keep the (rare) haircut small and the risk contained:

- **The channel already targets the paying geography.** Every idea-pool row is
  tagged for Spain / US-Hispanic / Europe reach ([brain/18](18-monetization-and-audience.md)) —
  the ideation isn't chasing cheap LATAM volume. An Ensayo episode inherits that.
- **The process is the transformation record.** `05-script.md` → `06-shotlist.md`
  → the sala (`09-timeline.json` + the `*.review.html` files) is a dated,
  complete account of the editorial work done to every clip. If a dispute ever
  needs a fair-use showing, it exists already — we don't have to reconstruct it.
- **[brain/11 §2.2](11-visual-rhythm.md) already caps beat duration.** B-roll beats
  run 4–11 s with a hard ⚠ at 20 s; only a-roll (our own footage) is uncapped. On
  the rare moving excerpt, it's B-roll and inherits that cap on top of §4.4.

Operating rules:

- **Score the episode with the haircut applied** — `01-brief.md` carries the mode
  and a note "RPM reducido (clips)"; the [rubric](../ideas/idea-rubric.md)
  monetisation tag is set one step below the category it would otherwise get.
  Justify the episode on P2/P4/P6, not on the CPM tag.
- **Disputing a Content ID claim can escalate to a takedown and a strike.**
  Dispute **only** where the commentary case is unambiguous. Otherwise let the
  claim stand — a revenue redirect on the clip portion of one video is survivable;
  a strike threatens the whole channel.
- **Jurisdiction, one line:** "fair use" is US doctrine; Spain/LATAM "derecho de
  cita" is narrower. YouTube runs US-style Content ID globally. Keep §4 tight;
  escalate a specific worry to a lawyer ([brain/04](04-legal-and-ethics.md)) — this is not legal advice.

## 7. Cadence cap

This only engages for the rare **moving-excerpt exception** (§4.1) — the normal
case (cited stills) isn't capped, since it carries essentially none of the
Content ID / RPM risk this cap exists for.

Until three episodes using a moving excerpt have published **and** been
reviewed at 48 h + 30 d (`tools/metrics.py`) **with their Content ID outcomes
recorded**: **≤ 1 in every 4 published episodes** uses one. After the third,
review: retention vs. the channel baseline, the Content ID claim rate, whether
any dispute was needed, the RPM hit. Then re-set the cap (raise, keep, or pause
the exception).

## 8. Still to build

The Ensayo track is live end to end for the normal case (cited stills) —
ideation, brief, outline, script, fact-check, and shotlist all have their
Ensayo handling (§6; the Whiplash demo, `episodes/_DEMO-ensayo-whiplash/`, is a
worked Stage-4 example). What's left is genuinely optional: `scene_locator.py`
(deprioritised) and the `brain/12 §1c` fold. `clip_cut.py` isn't being built —
see §6.
